# Fair Squares — Plain-English Housing Research Brief

**Purpose:** turn recent Dutch housing-market research into a practical story for Fair Squares.

**Focus:** why affordable housing projects often need a different kind of capital.

**Date:** 2026-05-14

---

## 1. The short version

The Dutch housing problem is not only that there are too few homes. It is also that many affordable homes are hard to finance.

Affordable rental projects usually earn less rent than market-rate projects. That is the point: the homes should stay affordable. But lower rent also means lower income for the project, less borrowing capacity and less room for investors who expect normal market returns. A project can therefore be good for society and still fail on a spreadsheet.

This is where Fair Squares has a credible role:

> **Fair Squares provides patient capital with capped returns for affordable housing projects that are socially valuable but underfinanced by the normal market.**

The goal is not to maximise profit from housing scarcity. The goal is to close a documented financing gap so affordable homes can actually be built, transformed or preserved.

## 3. What the research says, in normal language

### 3.1 More homes matter

CPB finds that many middle-income households are stuck. They earn too much for social housing, but not enough for an affordable market home. For them, the biggest help is not a perfect housing category. It is simply more homes.

Where land is scarce, smaller and more efficient homes can help more people than larger homes on the same land.

**What this means for Fair Squares:**  
Fund projects that add real homes efficiently: mid-rent units, transformations, building splits, rooftop additions and reuse of existing buildings.

### 3.2 Affordable rent can make a project harder to finance

Rent caps and affordability rules help tenants. But they also reduce the income a project can earn. If the income is lower, the project can borrow less and ordinary investors may walk away.

That does not mean rent caps are wrong. It means the financing stack has to change.

**What this means for Fair Squares:**  
Fair Squares can provide the lower-return capital layer that makes capped-rent projects possible.

### 3.3 Broad subsidies can miss the target

CPB warns that broad housing subsidies can have limited effect when land, permits and planning are the real bottlenecks. In that situation, money can leak into higher land prices instead of creating more affordable homes.

CPB is more positive about targeted support where a specific project has a real financial gap, for example when regulated rents do not cover development costs despite real demand.

**What this means for Fair Squares:**  
Do not fund vague ambitions. Fund a documented project gap. Require open-book budgets, fixed land/control terms and affordability covenants.

### 3.4 Housing corporations have a mission, but not unlimited capacity

Housing corporations can accept lower returns because they have a social mission. But they still depend on rental income and borrowing capacity. Lower rents can reduce the number of projects they can finance.

The Dutch government has also reported a large investment shortfall for the corporation sector.

**What this means for Fair Squares:**  
Fair Squares can complement municipalities, corporations and social operators with project-level capital. It should not claim to replace them.

### 3.5 Helping households borrow more is not the answer

CPB shows that buying a home has become much harder for median-income households. But simply increasing borrowing power can push prices higher and increase debt.

**What this means for Fair Squares:**  
Fair Squares should not frame itself as a way to help households take on more leverage. The better story is shared, capped-return capital that lowers the pressure to extract maximum rent or sales value.

### 3.6 Land and planning are real constraints

CPB also points to land scarcity, protected areas and slow planning as binding constraints. Fair Squares cannot solve planning law.

**What this means for Fair Squares:**  
Focus on projects where planning risk is already reduced, or where existing buildings can be transformed, split or topped up.

## 4. The financing problems Fair Squares can credibly solve

### Problem 1 — The affordable-rent viability gap

A project may work at market rent but fail at affordable rent. Fair Squares can fill the final layer of capital with a capped-return product.

Possible structure:

- junior equity, mezzanine or preferred equity;
- target return around 3–4%, depending on risk;
- long-term affordability covenant;
- open-book project budget;
- reporting on rent levels, occupancy and use of funds.

### Problem 2 — Balance-sheet limits at mission-driven operators

A housing corporation or social developer may have the right project and the right mission, but not enough investment room.

Fair Squares can provide project-level co-capital without taking over operations.

### Problem 3 — Subsidy or mission-capital leakage

If capital arrives before land terms are fixed, the benefit can be captured by landowners.

Fair Squares should require:

- fixed land/control terms before commitment;
- open-book development costs;
- transfer and refinancing restrictions;
- clawbacks if affordability is removed;
- capped investor upside.

### Problem 4 — The missing middle

Middle-income households often fall between social housing and expensive market housing.

Fair Squares can target mid-rent homes with income-aware allocation handled by trusted local partners.

## 5. Recommended public positioning

### One-liner

> **Fair Squares is a transparent, capped-return capital layer for affordable housing projects that are socially valuable but underfinanced by traditional markets.**

### Human explanation

Some affordable housing projects do not need a full subsidy. They need the last piece of patient capital that makes the numbers work without forcing rents back to market levels. Fair Squares is built to provide that layer, with capped investor returns and clear affordability commitments.

### What not to claim

Do not say Fair Squares will “solve the housing crisis.” That is too broad and not credible.

A stronger claim is:

- we help specific affordable projects move forward;
- we reduce dependence on high-return private capital;
- we protect affordability with covenants and reporting;
- we work alongside municipalities, corporations and social developers;
- we measure homes enabled, rent discounts and affordability years.

---

## 6. First pilot product

**Best first product:** viability-gap financing for mid-rent or affordable rental projects.

Fair Squares provides the final layer of patient capital so a project can proceed while rents stay capped.

**Second product:** transformation, building splits or rooftop additions.

These projects are attractive because they can add homes without depending as much on new land.

**Avoid at the start:** full property acquisition by Fair Squares.

That would be too operationally heavy for the first pilot.

---

## 7. Pilot selection criteria

A good first pilot should have:

1. A real location and credible local partner.
2. A clear affordability goal.
3. A documented financing gap.
4. Land/control terms already fixed or mostly de-risked.
5. Planning risk reduced enough that capital can turn into homes.
6. A long-term affordability covenant.
7. Clean reporting and governance rights.

---

## 8. Suggested pilot KPI set

- Affordable homes enabled per €1m of Fair Squares capital.
- Rent discount versus market or regulatory benchmark.
- Affordability years locked.
- Financing gap closed.
- Months saved compared with a delayed or cancelled project.
- Capital brought in alongside Fair Squares.
- Covenant compliance and reporting completeness.

---

## 9. How the current protocol fits

The existing Fair Squares prototype can still matter, but it should support the finance product rather than lead the story.

Useful protocol features:

- investor onboarding;
- capped-return accounting;
- transparent project reporting;
- governance rights;
- privacy-preserving eligibility checks;
- aggregate public data without exposing private investor details.

The message should be:

> The technology exists to make the capital layer transparent and accountable. The first job is to prove the legal and financial pilot.
