# Fair Squares — Production Path

## Goal

Turn Fair Squares from a protocol prototype into a credible pilot for affordable housing finance.

The production story should be simple enough for a municipality, housing corporation, social developer or social investor to understand in one minute:

> Some affordable housing projects are good projects, but the numbers do not work because affordable rents lower the return. Fair Squares provides patient capital with capped returns to close that gap.

## Website structure

1. **Home**  
   Explain the thesis in plain English: capped-return capital for affordable housing projects that the normal market leaves unfunded.

2. **Research**  
   Translate the Dutch research into clear English. Every source should answer two questions: what does it say, and what does it mean for Fair Squares?

3. **Pilot**  
   Show the first product: viability-gap financing for mid-rent or affordable rental projects. Include terms, partner checklist and success metrics.

4. **Fund / Invest / Governance**  
   Keep the protocol app available, but secondary. The protocol should support the real-world pilot, not distract from it.

## Claims we can safely make

- The Netherlands has a large housing shortage. ABF/Primos estimates the 2025 statistical shortage at roughly 396,000 homes.
- The Dutch government targets 900,000 new homes through 2030, with two-thirds intended to be affordable.
- CPB warns that broad construction subsidies can have limited effect if land and permits are the bottleneck.
- CPB also says targeted support can help when regulated rents make a specific project financially unviable.
- Rent caps can improve affordability for tenants, but they can also make rental supply harder to finance.
- Housing corporations have a social mission, but their investment capacity is limited.

## Product discipline

Fair Squares should not present itself as:

- a generic real-estate fund;
- a broad subsidy pool;
- a tokenization-first project;
- a promise to solve the entire housing crisis.

The first product should be narrower and more believable:

> **Capped-return viability-gap capital for specific affordable housing projects, deployed only where the gap is documented and affordability is locked.**

## Legal and structural work before launch

Resolve this before public fundraising:

- fund, cooperative, foundation, project company or loan-note model;
- AFM/AIFMD/licensing perimeter;
- retail versus qualified/social investor access;
- consumer and tenant protections;
- tax treatment of capped returns;
- governance rights and covenant enforceability;
- reporting duties and data privacy.

## Pilot KPI set

- Affordable homes enabled per €1m of Fair Squares capital.
- Rent discount versus local market or regulatory benchmark.
- Affordability years locked into the project.
- Documented financing gap closed.
- Months saved compared with a delayed or cancelled project.
- Extra capital brought in alongside Fair Squares.
- Covenant compliance and reporting completeness.

## Immediate next step

Find one real partner with one real project and ask:

> Do you have a socially desirable housing project that is blocked because affordability requirements make the return too low?

If the answer is yes, Fair Squares can test whether capped-return capital is enough to close the gap.
